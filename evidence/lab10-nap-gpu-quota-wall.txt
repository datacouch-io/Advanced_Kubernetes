=== NAP + GPU: real quota wall, live tested on real GKE ===

Cluster autoprovisioning updated to allow nvidia-tesla-t4 accelerators (min=1 max=1):
$ gcloud container clusters update advk8s-autoscale --enable-autoprovisioning \
    --min-accelerator=type=nvidia-tesla-t4,count=1 --max-accelerator=type=nvidia-tesla-t4,count=1

Deployed a pod requesting cpu=2, memory=4Gi, limits.nvidia.com/gpu=1

$ kubectl get events (sorted by time)
Warning  FailedScheduling   0/4 nodes are available: 3 Insufficient cpu, 3 Insufficient memory,
  4 Insufficient nvidia.com/gpu. no new claims to deallocate, preemption not helpful.
Normal   NotTriggerScaleUp  Pod didn't trigger scale-up: 2 Insufficient cpu, 2 Insufficient memory,
  2 Insufficient nvidia.com/gpu, 1 exceeded quota: "cluster-wide", resources: cpu, memory

Pod stayed Pending indefinitely. This matches the project-wide GPU quota finding from before
this lab was built (GCP project quota GPUS_ALL_REGIONS=0 even though individual GPU-type
regional buckets showed limit=1 -- the project-wide bucket is the actual binding constraint).

This is the real, live-verified behavior of hitting a GPU quota wall: NAP correctly identifies
that a new accelerator-backed node pool is needed and attempts to provision it, but the
underlying cloud quota blocks the compute instance from ever coming up, and the pod simply
stays Pending with an accurate, actionable error rather than failing silently or misleadingly.
The fix in a real environment is a GCP quota increase request (Console > IAM & Admin > Quotas),
not anything on the Kubernetes/NAP side -- NAP did its job correctly here.
